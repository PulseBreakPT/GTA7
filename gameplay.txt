GTA 6 Wanted Level & Police System
Grand Theft Auto VI significantly expands the traditional Wanted Level system. Police still respond according to a six-star scale, but the chase is no longer driven only by how many stars are visible on the HUD. Law enforcement can also gather information about the suspect, including physical appearance, clothing, vehicles, surveillance footage, companions and the location of a crime. The result is a system built around both police escalation and identification.
This distinction is important: Jason or Lucia can be wanted because a crime has been reported without necessarily being fully identified. Avoiding witnesses and cameras can therefore matter just as much as escaping the initial police response.

How the Wanted System Works
The Wanted Stars still communicate the seriousness of the current police response. More severe or escalating crimes can produce a stronger response, but GTA 6 adds a second layer: the police can build a profile of the suspect from the information available at the scene.
Known information can include the suspect’s appearance, outfit, vehicle, companion, CCTV evidence and crime location. Red information icons beneath the Wanted Stars communicate some of these details to the player. This gives the escape process a more tactical structure: losing line of sight is useful, but removing or invalidating the information the police possess can be equally important.
Six-Star Wanted Level
GTA 6 restores a six-star Wanted Level. The exact crime threshold and response assigned to each star have not yet been fully detailed, so a precise star-by-star police response chart should not be treated as final before release.
The key change is that the star count and the suspect profile operate together. A low-level incident may still be difficult to escape if police have an excellent description, while a reported crime may generate a response without identifying the perpetrator at all.
Police Heat and Suspect Information
The Heat system represents the information and attention generated by criminal activity. Rather than instantly forgetting the player once a chase ends, specific areas can remain “hot,” with continued patrol activity and a greater risk of renewed contact with law enforcement.

Police Information Icons
Physical Description
A human-silhouette icon indicates that police have a useful description of the suspect. This information can come from witnesses or other observations. Changing appearance can make that description outdated. A confirmed example is changing hairstyle, which can alter the physical description police are using.
Clothing
A clothes-hanger icon indicates that police know what the suspect is wearing. Clothing and physical appearance are tracked separately. Changing only a small accessory may not be enough to invalidate the entire outfit description, so a more complete clothing change can be more effective.
CCTV
A camera icon indicates that surveillance footage has contributed to the investigation. Security cameras therefore matter during robberies and other crimes: being recorded can provide information even if a civilian witness does not get a clear look at the player.
Vehicle
Police can identify the vehicle connected to a crime or escape. Remaining in a known vehicle makes it easier for law enforcement to maintain the trail. Abandoning it and switching to another vehicle can remove one of the strongest clues in an active search.
Companion
Police can identify that Jason and Lucia are operating together. If both protagonists are associated with the same incident, separating them can reduce the usefulness of that information and make the search less straightforward.
Witnesses, Alarms and Crime Reporting
Crimes are not automatically reported in every situation. A civilian witness, law-enforcement witness or alarm can trigger a report. If an offence occurs without anyone seeing it and without an alarm being activated, the police may have less information or may not receive an immediate useful description.
This makes positioning, timing and stealth meaningful before the Wanted Level even appears. Avoiding cameras, staying out of a police officer’s line of sight and controlling who can observe the crime can reduce the amount of information passed to dispatch.
Hollow Wanted Star
A hollow Wanted Star represents a reported crime where police are responding but do not yet have useful identifying information about the perpetrator. It is one of the clearest signs that GTA 6 separates “a crime has been reported” from “the police know who committed it.”
Police Dispatch
Dispatch can communicate specific details instead of only generating a generic police response. Reported information can include the suspect’s description, outfit, vehicle, companion, location and surveillance evidence. The more reliable information dispatch receives, the more targeted the search can become.
Changing Clothes and Appearance
Changing clothing can invalidate the outfit description being used by police, while changing hairstyle or other appearance details can affect the physical-description profile. Because these are separate categories, changing one does not necessarily remove the other.
Personal vehicles can carry spare outfits in the trunk, allowing Jason and Lucia to change clothing away from a safehouse or store. This connects the Wanted system directly with the vehicle inventory system and gives personal cars an additional role during criminal activity.
Switching Vehicles
If police have identified a vehicle, staying in it can preserve a strong link between the player and the crime. Switching cars while out of direct observation can therefore be an important part of an escape. This mechanic also interacts with the new vehicle-theft system, because the fastest getaway option may be an older low-security car that can be stolen quickly rather than an expensive vehicle with advanced anti-theft protection.
Masks and Concealing Identity
Masks can reduce the quality of a witness description, but they do not make the player completely anonymous. Police may still know the suspect’s clothes, vehicle, location, companion or CCTV trail. A mask is therefore one part of a broader identity-management system rather than an automatic way to erase Wanted information.
Hot Areas and Persistent Police Attention
Crime scenes and surrounding areas can remain hot after the immediate chase. These zones are visible on the map, and police can continue patrolling them. Returning too soon can therefore be risky even after the active search has weakened.
The system encourages players to move away from the crime scene, change identifiable details and allow police attention to cool instead of circling back immediately.
Splitting Up Jason and Lucia
When both protagonists are connected to the same crime, separating them can make the police profile less useful. This adds a cooperative dimension to escapes in story sequences involving both characters: staying together may be convenient, but it can also preserve a known link between the suspects.
Stealth and Line of Sight
Stealth has a larger role in avoiding identification. Jason has been shown crouching to stay outside a police officer’s line of sight, and the broader system rewards committing crimes without creating a clean witness description. A quiet escape can therefore prevent the Wanted system from escalating into a fully informed manhunt.
De-Escalation and Non-Lethal Choices
Not every confrontation needs to become a high-level pursuit. In a demonstrated scenario, threatening hostile NPCs with a firearm caused them to flee and report the incident, but the lack of gunfire kept the response comparatively limited. GTA 6’s combat systems also support intimidation, suppression and incapacitation, giving the player more options than immediately killing every threat.
Focus Ability and Police Searches
Police locations are more closely tied to the Focus ability than to traditional minimap icons. Focus can reveal nearby officers through walls, making it useful when moving through buildings, alleys or other areas where the player cannot see the entire search perimeter. This creates a direct connection between the Wanted system and the protagonist’s special ability.
How to Lose the Police
1. Escape the immediate line of sight and create distance from responding officers.
2. Check the information icons beneath the Wanted Stars to see what police know.
3. Change clothing if the outfit has been identified.
4. Change hairstyle or appearance if a physical description is active.
5. Abandon or switch vehicles if police know the current car.
6. Separate Jason and Lucia if both are linked to the crime.
7. Leave the hot area and avoid returning to the crime scene.
8. Wait for regional police attention to decrease.
9. Avoid giving witnesses or cameras useful information in the first place.

GTA 6 Vehicle Theft: Stealing, Selling & Keeping Cars
Vehicle theft has been redesigned into a progression system rather than a universal one-button action. Parked vehicles can have different security levels, modern cars can require specialist equipment, some stolen vehicles contain trackers, and players can evaluate a target before attempting the theft. Stolen cars can then be sold for money or converted into personal vehicles by removing security devices and registering them.

Parked Cars vs. Carjacking
The layered security system applies primarily to parked, unoccupied vehicles. Occupied vehicles can still be taken by pulling the driver out, preserving the classic GTA carjacking mechanic. However, passengers may resist, creating a short quick-time struggle instead of every hijacking playing out identically.
The Vehicle Theft Loop
1. Locate a potential target.
2. Scan the vehicle with the WAINK device when appropriate.
3. Check its security level, value, tracker status and registration cost.
4. Decide whether the available tools can bypass its protection.
5. Enter the vehicle using the required method.
6. Deal with any tracker or ongoing security risk.
7. Choose whether to sell the car or keep it.
8. If keeping it, remove the tracker when necessary and pay to register it as a personal vehicle.
WAINK Scanner
The WAINK scanner is a vehicle-assessment device used before or around a theft. It turns car stealing into a risk-versus-reward decision by providing information that would otherwise be hidden.
The scanner can reveal:
Vehicle security information
Estimated vehicle value
Whether a tracker is installed
The cost to register the vehicle as a personal car
A valuable modern car may produce a higher payout, but it can also require expensive equipment, take longer to steal and include tracking technology. Older or cheaper cars may be less profitable but faster and safer to take.
Vehicle Security Levels
Parked vehicles are divided into different security levels. The exact number of tiers has not yet been revealed, but security can affect how difficult a car is to enter, which tools are needed, how long the theft takes and whether extra anti-theft technology is present.
This also creates progression: as Jason and Lucia gain access to more advanced tools, vehicles that were previously impractical or impossible to steal become viable targets.
Breaking Into Older and Simpler Vehicles
Lower-security vehicles can still be taken with relatively direct methods. A slim jim can be used on compatible cars, while smashing a window remains another option. These methods are faster than sophisticated electronic bypasses, but they can create noise, visible damage or witness attention.
Digital Key Cloning
Modern vehicles can use electronic security that cannot be defeated by simply forcing a lock. A digital key-cloning device allows the player to bypass some of these systems. The tool is not freely available from the beginning: it must be obtained, it costs money, and more advanced theft equipment becomes available as the story and fence-related activities progress.
This turns vehicle theft into a small equipment economy. Better tools do not merely make existing thefts easier; they expand the range of vehicles the protagonists can realistically target.
Vehicle Trackers
Successfully entering a car does not always end the theft. Some vehicles contain tracking technology. More advanced trackers can reportedly expose the vehicle’s position to law enforcement under specific conditions, including when the car is being driven below a certain speed.
Tracker removal can eventually be unlocked through fences. Until that option becomes available, a high-value stolen car can remain a liability even after the player has escaped the original scene.
Selling Stolen Vehicles
Stolen cars can be taken to vehicle fences and sold for profit. This makes vehicle theft a direct money-making activity instead of only a way to obtain temporary transportation.
Resale value is influenced by the vehicle itself and can be reduced by damage. Tracker-equipped cars can also require additional work before they are ideal to sell. Exact sale prices and the full list of fence locations have not yet been revealed.
Keeping a Stolen Vehicle
Jason and Lucia can own multiple personal vehicles. To permanently convert a stolen car into a personal vehicle, the player needs to remove its tracker when one is fitted and pay the required registration cost. The WAINK scanner can display that cost before the player commits to keeping the car.
Unregistered vehicles are temporary and can eventually despawn. However, a stolen vehicle can be placed in a garage before registration, allowing the player to preserve a valuable find until the required tracker removal or registration process becomes available.
Garages and Personal Vehicle Storage
Residential safehouses are tied to story progression rather than being freely purchased, but separate garages can be bought around Leonida to increase vehicle storage. This means the permanent vehicle-ownership loop is not limited to whichever safehouse is currently active.
Personal vehicles also work as mobile storage. Their trunks can hold spare outfits and additional equipment, making an owned car useful for more than transportation.
Fuel and Battery State of Stolen Cars
A stolen car is not guaranteed to begin with a full tank or a full battery. Players can therefore take a vehicle only to discover that it is already low on range. This is particularly important during police escapes, where the easiest car to steal may not be the safest long-term getaway vehicle.
Vehicle Theft During Police Chases
The security system creates a practical trade-off during pursuits. A high-value modern vehicle may take too long to bypass, while a low-security car can be entered quickly and used to break identification. Because Leonida has denser traffic and narrower roads than Los Santos, improvising with whatever vehicle is available can be more useful than searching for the perfect car.
Theft Strategy
Scan valuable targets before committing to the theft.
Treat security level and tracker status as part of the vehicle’s real cost.
Use simple cars when speed matters more than resale value.
Avoid unnecessary damage if the plan is to sell the vehicle.
Upgrade theft tools to expand the pool of cars that can be targeted.
Use garages to preserve unregistered vehicles until they can be processed.
Remember that the police may identify both the stolen vehicle and the protagonists.

GTA 6 Fuel & Refuelling System
Grand Theft Auto VI introduces a functional fuel system for road vehicles. Cars and motorcycles consume fuel while being driven, gas stations can be used to refuel and repair vehicles, and electric vehicles use a dedicated charging system. The mechanic is designed to matter during travel and escapes without turning every drive into constant maintenance.

How Fuel Works
Vehicles lose fuel as they are driven. The system applies to normal road use rather than being a one-off mission gimmick, making fuel part of the broader vehicle simulation. It also connects directly to theft, because a car taken from the street may already be close to empty.
The exact consumption formula is not known. It has not yet been confirmed whether heavier, faster or more powerful vehicles consume fuel at different rates, or whether all combustion vehicles use a simplified common model.
Fuel Gauge and HUD
The fuel gauge is not intended to occupy the HUD permanently in the default presentation. It appears when the vehicle begins running low, reducing interface clutter during normal driving while still warning the player before the situation becomes critical.
The HUD is reported to offer substantial customization, so the final game may provide additional display options. The permanent visibility of the fuel gauge has not yet been confirmed.
Refuelling at Gas Stations
Gas stations serve as refuelling points. Preview information describes the process as a short interaction rather than a lengthy simulation. The player initiates refuelling and a brief cutscene handles the process, with the cost described as relatively modest.
A preview build used a hold interaction on the controller and the sequence lasted roughly ten seconds. Exact button prompts and timing should be treated as pre-release interface details rather than guaranteed final values.
Vehicle Repairs at Gas Stations
Gas stations can also repair vehicles. This creates convenient roadside service points where the player can restore both range and vehicle condition without necessarily visiting a dedicated customization shop or garage.
The final repair pricing, damage limits and whether heavily destroyed vehicles can still be serviced have not yet been detailed.
Electric Vehicles and Charging
Electric vehicles use a separate recharging system instead of conventional fuel. Charging stations are part of the world and provide the equivalent of refuelling for battery-powered cars.
The exact charging duration, battery-capacity differences and whether fast-charging locations exist have not yet been revealed. What is confirmed is that electric vehicles are integrated into the same broader range-management system rather than operating with unlimited energy.
Stolen Vehicles May Be Low on Fuel
Street vehicles do not spawn with guaranteed full fuel tanks or batteries. A newly stolen car can therefore have limited remaining range. This detail links the fuel mechanic to the redesigned theft system and makes the WAINK/security decision only one part of choosing a getaway vehicle.
Fuel During Police Chases
Running low during a pursuit creates a new type of pressure. A player escaping in an unfamiliar stolen car may need to decide whether to keep driving, switch vehicles or risk stopping at a service point. Since police can also identify vehicles, abandoning a low-fuel car can solve both a range problem and an identification problem at the same time.
Long-Distance Travel
Leonida spans dense urban roads, coastal areas, wetlands and more remote regions. Fuel makes long-distance travel slightly more deliberate, particularly when using a stolen vehicle with an unknown starting level. At the same time, the mechanic is designed so that normal vehicles can travel for a substantial period before refuelling is required.
What Happens When Fuel Reaches Zero?
The final behavior of a completely empty fuel tank has not been fully detailed. The obvious possibility is that the engine stops and the vehicle becomes unusable until refuelled, but recovery mechanics remain unknown.
Unanswered questions include whether fuel cans can be carried, whether roadside assistance exists, whether a personal vehicle can be recovered remotely and how electric vehicles are handled when their battery reaches zero far from a charger.
Fuel and GTA 6 Online
No final fuel rules have been announced for the next GTA Online experience. The single-player system should not automatically be assumed to carry over unchanged. Multiplayer balance, griefing and the concentration of players around fuel stations could require different rules.

GTA 6 Focus Ability
Focus is a new multi-purpose ability in Grand Theft Auto VI. It slows the action, highlights useful targets and information, and supports combat, police evasion and robberies. Although it has obvious similarities to Red Dead Redemption 2’s Dead Eye, Focus is broader than a simple slow-motion aiming mode.

How Focus Works
Activating Focus slows the surrounding action and gives the player a short period to assess threats or important objects. The ability is governed by a visible purple meter. Once the available Focus is exhausted, the slow-motion effect ends and gameplay returns to normal speed.
Because the same resource can be useful in several situations, timing becomes important. Spending Focus early in an easy encounter can leave less available for a difficult shootout, a police search or a robbery where identifying the most valuable loot matters.
Enemy Body-Part Targeting
Focus can highlight specific areas of an enemy’s body. Red target areas indicate lethal shot placement, while yellow areas indicate non-lethal or incapacitating options. This supports GTA 6’s wider combat design, where shooting limbs can disable an opponent without necessarily killing them.
The system gives the player greater control over the outcome of a firefight. A head or upper-body shot can be used for maximum lethality, while targeting an arm or leg can neutralize a threat with a lower chance of killing the target.
Non-Lethal Combat and Suppression
Focus works alongside a broader set of non-lethal combat mechanics. NPCs can be intimidated, disarmed or frightened by suppression fire, and incapacitated enemies do not always need to be finished off. This matters because unnecessary violence can also affect police escalation and the game’s wider criminal-behavior systems.
Vehicle Component Targeting
Focus can identify important vehicle components including the engine block, fuel tank and tyres. Instead of simply firing repeatedly at a vehicle, players can choose a specific mechanical objective: disable mobility, damage the engine or target the fuel system.
This mechanic becomes especially interesting when combined with the new fuel simulation and more detailed vehicle damage. Precise targeting can potentially stop a fleeing car without requiring the player to destroy it outright.
Finding Police Through Walls
Police officers are less dependent on the traditional minimap presentation. Focus can reveal nearby officers through walls, helping the player understand a search perimeter when direct line of sight is blocked.
This makes Focus useful during wanted situations inside buildings, alleys, back rooms and dense urban spaces. Instead of simply watching police dots on the minimap, the player can actively use the ability to read the environment before moving.
Focus During Robberies
Focus can highlight valuable objects during robberies. In locations such as jewelry stores, this allows the player to identify worthwhile targets quickly rather than looting every object indiscriminately.
The ability can also support the broader planning process by highlighting points of interest and useful escape information. This links the special ability directly to GTA 6’s more systemic robbery design.
Points of Interest and Escape Routes
Focus is not restricted to combat targets. It can identify useful environmental information, including points of interest and possible escape routes. This makes it closer to an awareness mode than a traditional combat-only superpower.
Focus Meter and Resource Management
The purple bar represents the amount of Focus currently available. The meter is finite, so the ability cannot be treated as permanent slow motion. Exactly how quickly the meter drains, how quickly it naturally regenerates and whether consumables restore it have not yet been fully explained.
Cinematic Slow Motion
GTA 6 also uses dramatic slow-motion presentation during certain combat moments. This cinematic effect is related to the game’s broader emphasis on readable, physical gunfights, but it should not be assumed that every cinematic slowdown consumes the player-controlled Focus meter.
Focus vs. Dead Eye
The closest comparison is Dead Eye from Red Dead Redemption 2 because both systems slow time and help the player make precise decisions. The major difference is scope: Focus is also tied to police awareness, environmental information, robbery loot and vehicle components. It functions as a general tactical-awareness ability rather than only a precision-shooting mode.

GTA 6 Robberies & Money System
Robberies are integrated deeply into GTA 6’s open world and economy. Small businesses can be robbed opportunistically, larger targets can appear as higher-value opportunities, NPCs can provide tips, safes may require combinations, stolen goods often need to be fenced, and money is divided between banked funds, cash carried on the character and the value of loot currently held in bags.
The system is designed to make earning, carrying and converting money part of the criminal loop rather than treating every robbery as an instant cash reward.

Almost Any Store Can Become a Target
GTA 6 gives the player far more freedom to create robberies without waiting for a scripted mission marker. Small businesses such as convenience stores and phone-repair shops can be held up as low-tier targets. Preview information places some of these smaller robberies around the $1,000 range, although exact payouts should be treated as pre-release balancing rather than fixed final values.
These smaller opportunities are not necessarily marked on the map. A player can discover a suitable store while exploring, decide to rob it and deal with the consequences dynamically.
Major Robbery Targets
Higher-value robbery opportunities include jewelry stores, banks, compounds and certain vans. These can be surfaced more deliberately, including map information that indicates an estimated robbery value. Larger targets naturally involve greater security, more complicated access and a higher risk of police attention.
Tips, Eavesdropping and Leads
Robbery opportunities are not discovered only through icons. NPCs can provide anonymous tips, and Jason or Lucia can learn about possible scores by listening to conversations or following leads. This makes exploration and social interaction part of the process of finding profitable targets.
Money Is Split Into Multiple Forms
GTA 6 distinguishes between money safely stored in a bank account, physical cash carried by the player and the estimated value of stolen goods inside a duffle bag. This means “having loot” and “having spendable money” are not always the same thing.
The distinction creates risk after the robbery. Valuable items may still need to be sold, while carried cash may need to be deposited before it is fully protected from the consequences of being caught.
Bank Money
Banked money represents funds that have already been secured. The interface can display bank funds separately from cash on hand, making it easier to understand which money is safely stored and which remains exposed during criminal activity.
Cash on Hand
Physical cash is immediately usable but remains part of the money carried by the character. The full penalties for being arrested or otherwise losing a robbery have not yet been completely detailed.
Loot Value
Jewelry, drugs and other stolen goods can have an estimated value without being direct cash. These items are carried as loot and often require a fence before their value becomes spendable money.
Duffle Bags and Loot Capacity
Loot is physically represented through bags. Jason and Lucia can carry multiple loot bags, and additional bags can be stored in vehicle trunks. This turns the getaway vehicle into part of the robbery logistics: the trunk can hold extra loot, weapons, equipment and spare clothing.
The final bag-capacity rules, weight limits and whether different bags have different capacities have not yet been fully disclosed.
Safes and Combinations
Some robberies involve safes rather than a simple cash register. Opening them can require a combination, and obtaining that combination may involve searching the environment for clues or getting information from NPCs. The robbery therefore becomes a small investigation instead of only a hold-up.
Security Cameras and Backdoors
CCTV is relevant both to the robbery itself and to the Wanted system. Being recorded can give police better identification information. Backdoors and alternate exits can provide escape options that avoid the most obvious police approach or help the player leave without crossing the same public area used to enter.
Dynamic NPC Reactions
Bystanders are not guaranteed to behave passively. Some NPCs may flee, report the crime or attempt to intervene. A robbery can therefore change unexpectedly depending on who is inside the location and how aggressively the player behaves.
This creates an important decision between speed and control. Spending too long searching for every valuable item can increase the chance of witnesses, resistance, alarms or police arrival.
Focus Ability During Robberies
Focus can identify valuable objects and other useful points of interest. In a jewelry store or another loot-heavy location, it helps prioritize high-value targets instead of taking everything indiscriminately. Focus can also support situational awareness when police are approaching.
Fences and Converting Loot Into Cash
Not every stolen item has immediate cash value. Jewelry, drugs and other valuables can need to be taken to a fence. Only after the goods are sold does their value become normal money the protagonists can use.
This creates an additional vulnerable stage after the initial robbery. Escaping the store successfully is not necessarily the end of the job; the player may still need to transport the loot safely, find a fence and secure the money afterward.
Risk After the Robbery
A robbery can be technically successful while still producing little or no lasting profit if the player is caught before stolen goods are fenced or before vulnerable cash is secured. This extends the risk-and-reward loop beyond the front door of the target.
Robberies and the Wanted System
Robbery design is tightly connected to police identification. Witnesses can report the crime, alarms can trigger law enforcement, CCTV can provide identifying information, masks can reduce the quality of descriptions, and escape vehicles can themselves become known to police.
A careful robbery therefore involves more than grabbing money quickly: the player can also consider surveillance, witnesses, clothing, getaway vehicles, backdoors and the amount of information that will be passed to dispatch.
Money and Story Progression
Money has a more direct relationship with progression than in many earlier GTA stories. At certain points, insufficient funds can prevent the story from immediately moving forward, encouraging the player to use robberies and other money-making opportunities around Leonida before continuing.
New contacts and activities can open additional income sources as the story develops, so small store robberies are only one part of a larger criminal economy.
Other Loot Opportunities
The wider open world includes additional objects that can be broken into or looted. Shipping containers can contain cash, weapons, supplies and even vehicles such as bikes. These discoveries reinforce the same principle as robberies: exploration can produce practical rewards without requiring a traditional mission start point.
A Typical Robbery Flow
1. Discover a target naturally, through the map, from an NPC tip or by overhearing information.
2. Assess entrances, cameras, witnesses and possible escape routes.
3. Choose clothing, mask, weapons, equipment and a getaway vehicle.
4. Enter the location and control or intimidate the people inside.
5. Take cash or use Focus to prioritize valuable objects.
6. Search for safe combinations or other access information if required.
7. Use a backdoor or another route to escape when useful.
8. Manage the Wanted Level and remove identifying information.
9. Move loot using duffle bags and vehicle storage.
10. Sell valuables to a fence and secure the resulting cash.

How the Five Systems Connect
These mechanics are designed to overlap rather than exist as isolated features. A robbery can generate witnesses and CCTV evidence; that evidence can create a detailed police profile; the player may escape in a stolen vehicle; the stolen vehicle can contain a tracker and a partially empty tank; Focus can reveal officers or important targets; and the stolen goods may still need to reach a fence before the money is safe. GTA 6’s core criminal gameplay is therefore built around chains of consequences instead of single disconnected actions.
